Below I am giving one page of that book: the Telugu translation, and the original English of the same page.

This Telugu has ALREADY been translated and proofed by another AI. So it is usually fluent on the surface, and the problems that are left tend to be quieter ones: a meaning that shifted, a detail that was dropped, a phrase that reads correctly but says the wrong thing. Hold a high bar. Flag what a careful editor would actually change - not mere preferences, and not things that are already fine.

A single word is a valid item. On a fluent page most of what an editor changes is one word: a word in the wrong sense, a word that is not this book's term (see THIS BOOK'S TERMS), a word weaker or stronger than the English, or a vague stand-in for a specific English word. These are errors, not preferences - flag each one on its own, as a one- or two-word item, without widening it to the sentence.
What IS a mere preference: swapping a correct word for a near-synonym only because it is closer to the English or has a slightly different shade. Flag a word only when you can say what a Telugu reader would misread, find odd, or miss in it here. Two sentences that each have a problem are two items, not one - and so are two separate problems in one sentence, unless they share words. A wrong word and an added word a few words apart (మమేకం for "involve", and a లోపలికి the English does not have) are two short items, not one clause-long item. Word-level items come on top of the sentence-level checks, never instead of them: every sentence still gets the meaning check (types 10 to 13) and the grammar check (type 16), and a page with many word fixes can still have a sentence that says the wrong thing. A sentence-level item is still marked on the words that carry its problem - the verb with the wrong mood, the connective, the clause that is missing or wrong - and covers the whole sentence only when the fix truly needs the whole sentence rebuilt (broken flow, English word order, a meaning that has to be said again).

The page is one piece of a longer book, cut at page boundaries. Its first or last sentence may start or end part way through. Do not flag a sentence only because it is cut off at the start or the end of the page.

Detection:

Note: do this in three passes, and work through all of them before you answer.

Pass 1 - THE TELUGU ON ITS OWN. Do not look at the English yet. Read the page the way a Telugu reader would, and identify where the reading stumbles:

1. Flow
Identify the sentences that read like broken chunks or are not properly closed. Often Sadhguru first states the main point and then adds to it, or says one long sentence that adds detail after detail before it closes. When a translation keeps every segment in the English order, the Telugu can read as a string of fragments. Identify these.

2. Intention
Identify the sentences or phrases that seem like the odd man in the room - they do not naturally say something related to the context around them. These are usually literal translations that do not carry what the speaker actually meant.

3. Generic words in place of contextually rich words
A word that is correct but flat, where a more specific word would convey what is actually meant.
Watch especially for these Telugu stand-ins. Each often sits where the English says something more specific - read the English and check:
- స్థితి - the English may say arena, dimension, level, situation or space, or have no "state" at all ("beyond the physical" is భౌతికమైన దానికి, not భౌతిక స్థితికి; "from one dimension to the other" is ఒక పార్శ్వం నుండి మరొక పార్శ్వానికి)
- సాధ్యపడు - work, open up, happen ("it did not work here" is వర్కవుట్ కాలేదు; "it will not open up" is తెరుచుకోదు)
- శక్తి - empowerment (సాధికారత), faculties
- జోక్యం - intrude (చొరబడటం)
- మమేకం - involve (నిమగ్నమవ్వడం), identify (గుర్తింపు ఏర్పరచుకోవడం) and relate are three different things
- an everyday word for one of Sadhguru's concepts: "knowing will never happen through a book" is about realisation - సాక్షాత్కారం, not తెలుసుకోవడం
- రాశి ("heap") is not a catch-all for Sadhguru's karma words: "karmic structure" కర్మ నిర్మాణం, "a body of memory" స్మృతి భాండాగారం, "stock" నిల్వ, "an accumulation" కూడబెట్టడం, "a catalog" జాబితా

4. An ending that is not connected to the rest of the sentence
The last part or phrase of the sentence does not connect to what came before it in the sentence.

5. Register
This is a book, and its register follows what is being said.
- Teaching passages: clear, modern written Telugu. Flag rare, learned words a general reader would stumble on (కృచ్ఛ్రసాధన, ప్రక్షేపణం, ఆత్మాశ్రయ, ఉన్మాద స్థితి, సమ్మిశ్రిత, సర్వజనీన, సంవేదన, దార్ఢ్యం, ఆయామం, దిగ్భ్రమ, తదుపరి, అనునిత్యం, రూపరహిత) in favour of a word in common use. Sadhguru's concept terms - THIS BOOK'S TERMS, and words like సాన్నిధ్యం, సాక్షాత్కారం, తీక్షణత - are not Grandhika even though they come from Sanskrit. Never flag them as formal, and never trade them for a plainer word that loses the concept.
- Stories, jokes, quoted dialogue, the questioner's words and Sadhguru's asides: everyday Telugu. A formal word or a long formal paraphrase there is the error. "emotionally blackmail her" is "ఎమోషనల్‌గా బ్లాక్‌మెయిల్ చేసేవాళ్ళం", not "భావోద్వేగాలతో లొంగదీసుకోవాలని చూసేవాళ్ళం". Where Telugu speakers use the English word itself - జోక్, ప్రోగ్రామ్, క్లాసు, బిజీ, మిస్ అవ్వడం, మూడ్, హ్యాండిల్, వర్కవుట్ - a stiff Telugu coinage (హాస్యాస్పదం for "a joke") is the error. Everyday Telugu is about the words, not the pronoun: Sadhguru's "you" stays మీరు (type 15).
- Never crude or slangy, even in a story.
- Anywhere on the page, a modern or technical thing that Telugu readers know by its English name is written in English, in Telugu script: ప్రోగ్రామ్ (an Isha program), మైక్రోస్కోప్, జెనెటిక్స్, ఫిట్‌నెస్, ఫాస్ట్-ఫార్వర్డ్.
- Do not bring in an English word for a spiritual or abstract idea, unless Telugu has no word that keeps a distinction Sadhguru is making (react, as against respond).

6. Contextual phrasing and action shifts
Phrases where a direct translation slightly shifts the meaning, emphasis or idiom of the action.
Example: "Great storyteller" translated as "గొప్ప కథలు చెప్పేవారు" (teller of great stories) misses the action. It should be flagged to change to "కథలు గొప్పగా చెప్పేవారు" (telling stories greatly).
Example: "Never be the same" translated as "ఒకేలా ఉండదు" (will not be identical) should be flagged to change to "మునుపటిలా ఉండదు" (will not be like before).
Images work in both directions.
- Keep Sadhguru's image when Telugu can carry it: "can you ride it a little bit?" - "దానిపై కాస్త స్వారీ చేయగలరా?"; "if your doors are open" - "మీ ద్వారాలు తెరిచి ఉంటే"; "I am poking at them" - "గిల్లుతూనే"; "throw yourself into the river" - "విసిరేసుకుంటే".
- When the literal image is foreign in Telugu, use the Telugu idiom: "the carrot and stick" - "సామదండాలు"; "resented this" - "జీర్ణించుకోలేకపోయేవారు"; "I am taking you for a ride" - "ఏమారుస్తున్నాను".
- Flag either kind when it has been flattened into a plain verb or replaced by an interpretation: "మీ అంతరంగం సిద్ధంగా ఉంటే" for "if your doors are open", "విమర్శిస్తూనే" for "poking", "సాధ్యపడదు" for "it will not open up".
Set phrases. A fixed English phrase is one unit of meaning, not a string of words - say what it means the way Telugu says it:
- "This is far from the truth" - ఏమాత్రం నిజం కాదు; "the jaws of death" - మృత్యు కోరలు; "somewhere along the way" - కాలక్రమంలో; "ninety-five percent of the time" - 95 శాతం సందర్భాల్లో
- "the moment X" - X-న మరుక్షణం; "where is the question of X" - X ప్రసక్తే లేదు; "in the process" - ఆ క్రమంలో, not ఆ ప్రక్రియలో; "in terms of X" - X విషయానికొస్తే, not X పరంగా; "it does not matter" - ముఖ్యం కాదు
- "Can you see…?" - చూశారా…?; "sit back defeated" - చేతులెత్తేసి కూర్చోవడం; "shatter illusions" - భ్రమలను పటాపంచలు చేయడం
- "there is no way to X" means X cannot happen - X సాధ్యం కాదు. "X-కు వేరే మార్గం లేదు" says the opposite: there is no OTHER way.
- an English repetition ("waited and waited") is a Telugu reduplication with its result - ఎదురుచూసి చూసి అలసిపోయారు. That result is not an addition.

7. A dictionary word where Telugu uses a different one (colloquial word, not the literal one)
The Telugu uses the word-for-word dictionary equivalent of the English word, but in this context a Telugu speaker would naturally use a different word. The word is not wrong in general - it is wrong here. Work out from the context what is actually being referred to, then ask what Telugu calls THAT. This is not the same as type 3: there the word is right but flat; here it is the wrong word for this context.
Example: "snakes and their symbols", where the symbols are carved or installed images of snakes, translated as "పాముల గుర్తులు". గుర్తులు means marks or signs; for images of a snake Telugu says "విగ్రహాలు" or "మూర్తులు".
More of the same kind - a word with several senses, given in its first dictionary sense. This is as common with abstract nouns, adjectives and verbs as with objects:
- "a hundred times more sterile" - పరిశుద్ధంగా (germ-free), not పవిత్రంగా (holy)
- "total calm" - ప్రశాంతత, not నిశ్శబ్దం
- "used in many different, very loose ways" - బాధ్యతారహితంగా, not అస్పష్టంగా
- "he could not refuse the offer" (to take his place) - ఆ సహాయాన్ని, not ఆ ప్రతిపాదనను
- "the reminder comes" - గుర్తు, not హెచ్చరిక
- "what does it cost to get married?" - మూల్యం, not ఖర్చు
- "a bear trap" - ఉచ్చు, not బోను
- "the consequence" - పర్యవసానం / పరిణామం, not ఫలితం (result)
- "incomplete" - అసంపూర్తి (unfinished), not అసంపూర్ణం (imperfect)
- "absurd" - హాస్యాస్పదం; "nonsense" - పనికిమాలిన; not అర్థరహితం (meaningless)
- "physical" meaning bodily (pleasure, pain, energy) - శారీరక, not భౌతిక
- a child who acts (chooses its parents, dies, goes to school) - పిల్లాడు, పిల్లలు; బిడ్డ is a baby
- "Once this distinction comes" (between you and your body) - తేడా, not విభజన
- "I see that it is not dissolved" (meaning I know) - తెలుసు, not చూస్తున్నాను
- "a little space between you and your body" - ఎడం, not శూన్యం
- "ensure" - జరిగేలా చూడటం, not నిర్ధారించడం
- "patterns" (of behaviour, karma) - ధోరణులు / పోకడలు, not పద్ధతులు
- "goes unexplored" - ఎవరూ అన్వేషించరు, not ఎవరూ స్పృశించరు ("no one touches it")
- "if your energies predominate in these centres" - ప్రబలమైతే, not ఆధిపత్యం వహిస్తే (political dominance)
One word in the wrong sense is enough to flag.

8. Context-appropriate synonyms
Of two near-synonyms, the Telugu has used the one that does not fit how Telugu says it in this context. Watch especially the everyday verbs - of saying, telling, calling, knowing, seeing, making, doing - where English uses one word and Telugu chooses between several.
Example: "they say" in the sense of how something is called, or what people commonly hold to be true, is "అంటున్నారు" / "అంటారు" - not "చెప్తున్నారు" / "చెప్తారు". చెప్పడం is telling something to someone; అనడం is how a name, a saying or a common belief is reported.
Other everyday verbs where English uses one word and Telugu chooses:
- requested, pleaded, prayed - in a story, అన్నాడు, వేడుకున్నాడు, not అభ్యర్థించాడు, ప్రార్థించాడు
- answer - బదులివ్వడం, not స్పందించడం
- know a person - పరిచయం ఉండటం; "you will not get it" - బోధపడదు
- feel, experience (Sadhguru, an energy, life, pain, an inner state) - అనుభూతి చెందడం, not అనుభవించడం or స్పర్శించడం
- look into, look at, go through - పరిశీలించడం
- remember (your being) - గుర్తుచేసుకోవడం, not గుర్తుంచుకోవడం
- struggling with - సతమతమవ్వడం, not పోరాడటం
- "you will find / notice / see that X" - …అని మీకు తెలుస్తుంది / అర్థమవుతుంది, not మీరు గమనిస్తారు / చూస్తారు; "there seems to be" - తోస్తోంది, not కనిపిస్తోంది; "would sound like a fairytale" - అనిపించవచ్చు
- determine the nature of something - నిర్దేశించడం; decide whether - నిర్ణయించడం
- "they say" reporting a finding (researchers say) - చెబుతున్నారు; అంటారు stays for sayings and names
Quotations: the verb that reports a quotation comes after it, with అని - "…" అని అన్నాడు, "…" అని బతిమాలాడు. Flag "X ఇలా అన్నాడు, "…"" and a quotation left with no verb after it. The verb that closes a quotation or an అని-clause is a form of అనడం (అన్నాడు, అంటూ, అని అంటారు), not చెప్పడం. "When we say X" is "X అన్నప్పుడు".


9. English sentence structure carried into the Telugu
The words are right, but they stand in the English order, so the sentence reads like a translation. Telugu has its own structure and it comes first: what qualifies something - the reason, cause, condition, time, place or manner - usually comes before what it qualifies, and the main point settles at the end with the verb.
Example: "This is very important for many reasons" translated as "ఇది అనేక కారణాల వల్ల చాలా ముఖ్యమైనది" keeps the English order. Telugu says "అనేక కారణాల వల్ల ఇది చాలా ముఖ్యమైనది".
But a sentence does not stand alone. Keep its joins with its neighbours intact: what links back to the previous sentence stays near the start, and what the next sentence picks up stays where the next sentence can reach it. Do not reorder a sentence into one that reads well by itself but no longer flows from the sentence before or into the sentence after - when the two pull against each other, find the order that satisfies both.
Flag only when the English order actually makes the Telugu read like a translation - not when it is merely one of several natural Telugu orders.
Other English shapes that read as translation:
- "There are some X who…" - కొందరు X ఉంటారు, వారు…
- "He knows that…", "Modern physics says that…" - the knower or sayer first, and the verb of knowing or saying once, at the end.
- an English abstract noun or -ly adverb that Telugu says with a verb: "a helpless dependence" - నిస్సహాయంగా ఆధారపడటం.
- an English ellipsis left incomplete: "Shiva can contain it, not you" - "…మీరు అలా చేయలేరు".
- "moment to moment" - క్షణం క్షణం, not క్షణం నుండి క్షణానికి.
- a list whose last item the English joins with "and": write the join - ఇంకా, అలాగే, or -ఊ … -ఊ for a pair (జ్ఞాపకశక్తి, ఊహాశక్తుల - జ్ఞాపకశక్తి ఇంకా ఊహాశక్తుల). A bare comma reads broken. "again" meaning "also" is అలాగే, not మళ్ళీ.
- an English passive that names its doer, or a -బడు passive where Telugu has its own verb or the doer is people in general: make the doer the subject, or use the Telugu verb ("is determined by your past patterns" - మీ గత ధోరణులే నిర్ణయిస్తున్నాయి; "is seen as" - …అని భావిస్తారు; "is molded" - రూపుదిద్దుకుంది). Leave the passives Telugu uses itself (అని పిలవబడే).
- "you have / become more X" for a state or a faculty takes the మీకు form - మీకు ఎరుక పెరుగుతుంది, మీకు నిద్ర పట్టదు; "as you become more and more X" - X పెరిగే కొద్దీ.
- an action done to or for oneself takes -కొను (సృష్టించుకుంటారు, వదిలించుకోవచ్చు); two states at once take -తూ (ఆహ్లాదంగా ఉంటూ, not ఉండి).
- "It is not that X" - X అని కాదు; "Once you X" - ఒక్కసారి X-ారంటే; "has a way of", "has" - …ఉంది, not …కలిగి ఉంది.
- "X or Y", "whether X or Y", X and Y after a negative - X-ఓ… Y-ఓ, X గానీ… Y గానీ, X-నా సరే… Y-నా సరే; not X లేదా Y.
- "Do X, and Y", "all it takes is X" - X చేస్తే (చాలు), Y.
- one root for a word and its opposite: "form" and "formless" - రూపం, రూపం లేని; "bound" and "boundless" - హద్దులు ఉండటం, హద్దులు లేకపోవడం.

14. English grammar words carried into the Telugu
Telugu leaves out what English grammar has to say. Flag these; the fix is to delete:
- a subject pronoun or possessive (మీరు, వారు, అతను, మనం, మీ, తన) that the verb ending or the context already gives
- ఒక for English "a" (ఒక సన్యాసి దాదాపు ఎప్పుడూ ఆకలితోనే ఉంటాడు - సన్యాసి దాదాపు ఎప్పుడూ…)
- ఆ / ఈ for "the" / "this" where nothing is being pointed at
- అక్కడ / ఇక్కడ from "there is" / "here is" - "There is no 'you' right now" is ప్రస్తుతం "మీరు" అనేదే లేదు, not ప్రస్తుతం అక్కడ "మీరు" అనేదే లేదు
- a reflexive written twice where it reads doubled (మిమ్మల్ని మీరు మీ అంతిమ పరిమితి వరకు నెట్టుకోండి - మీ అంతిమ పరిమితి వరకు నెట్టుకోండి). మిమ్మల్ని మీరు with a -కొను verb is often natural - flag it only where the sentence stumbles on it.
- spoken fillers translated word for word: "just", "simply", "you know", "then" after an if-clause, "I think" in a remark. Keep కేవలం only where "just" means "merely"; "just" meaning "for no reason" is ఊరికే, "that is all" is …అంతే, "easily" is ఇట్టే.
- యొక్క for "of" or "'s": Telugu joins the nouns directly (ఆ వ్యక్తి యొక్క కర్మ - ఆ వ్యక్తి కర్మ), makes a compound (శరీరం యొక్క పరిమాణం - శరీర పరిమాణం), or uses -పు, తాలూకు or a case ending (ఒక వ్యక్తి యొక్క - ఒకరి). Keep it only where a word between the two nouns would otherwise attach to the wrong one.
- English generic "one", "a person", "a child", "humans": no ఒక వ్యక్తి, ఒకరు, ఒక బిడ్డ. Keep the passage's మీరు, or say it generically (ఎవరినైనా, పిల్లల్లో, మన, మనిషి with a singular verb). Never turn Sadhguru's "you" into ఆ వ్యక్తి.
- "anything" after a negative - ఏదీ / ఏమీ; "something" - ఏదో; ఏదైనా means "any at all".

15. Respect and person
- When Sadhguru says "you" - to the reader, to his listeners, to a questioner, or "you" meaning anyone - it is మీరు, with its forms (మీ, మిమ్మల్ని, మీకు, మీరే) and verb endings (చేస్తారు, చేయండి). This holds almost without exception: in teaching, in stories and in jokes alike. Never flag a మీరు form, and never change one into నువ్వు, నీ, నిన్ను or a నువ్వు verb form (చేస్తావు, చెయ్యి) - not in detection, not in any version. Where the Telugu has నువ్వు or నీ for Sadhguru's own "you", flag it: the fix is మీరు.
- The only నువ్వు to leave alone is one the translation already has inside a story, between its own characters (a man to his wife, a parent to a child, friends). A quoted speech keeps one form throughout. Anyone addressing a king, a god, a Guru or an elder - మీరు. Never bring in a new నువ్వు for Sadhguru's "you".
- An ordinary seeker or person in the third person is అతను or ఆమె; Gurus, saints and Sadhguru are ఆయన. Characters in a story or joke - a driver, a policeman, a footballer, any famous person who is not a spiritual figure - are అతను, with -ాడు verbs (అన్నాడు, not అన్నారు). Leave Shankaran Pillai as the page has him.
- Sadhguru's "we", "our", "us": మనం / మన / మనకు in general statements; మేము / మా / మాకు when he speaks about himself or a specific group of people - Isha, the programs, his teachers, the yogic tradition.
- Goddesses take దేవి (పార్వతీ దేవి). A woman who asks a question is సాధకురాలు, not సాధకుడు. No గారు after a name or title the English gives bare (రామకృష్ణ పరమహంస గారు, పోప్ గారు) - the verb ending already carries the respect.

16. Spelling and grammar
A misspelt word, a word split by a space (చూసి నప్పుడు), or two words run together is its own item, with the corrected spelling as every version. Never bundle a spelling fix into a wording change - if the wording is rejected, the spelling fix is lost with it. An English gloss in brackets never separates a word from its suffix, and an English loan word is written together with its suffix (టెంట్‌లో).
Also check these forms, which read smoothly but are wrong: a noun describing another noun takes its oblique form (రాతి బుడగ, not రాయి బుడగ); a noun in -ం keeps it before a suffix (ధ్యానలింగంలో, not ధ్యానలింగలో); తనంతట తానుగా is for a person - for a thing, దానంతట అదే / వాటంతట అవే; animals are neuter (రెండూ ఒకదానికొకటి); the verb agrees with "a person" even where the English says "they"; a loan word takes one plural (రెసిడెంట్లు, not రెసిడెంట్సులు); నేను takes కాను, not కాదు.
A word, sentence or paragraph that appears twice in a row, the second copy adding nothing, is its own item - delete the repeat, even when the English itself has it twice (a leftover from editing). Sadhguru's deliberate repetition for emphasis stays.
A sentence left unfinished - an అని, an …ఏమిటంటే or a quotation that never closes - is its own item, and so is a wrong case ending (ఒకదానితో మరొకటి కొట్టాలి - ఒకదాన్ని మరొకదానితో కొట్టాలి).

Pass 2 - THE TELUGU AGAINST THE ENGLISH. Now read the English, and check the Telugu against it, sentence by sentence. This pass is for what the Telugu alone cannot show you:

10. Omissions
A clause, qualifier, example, name, number or detail that is in the English and missing from the Telugu - especially small words that change the sense, such as "only", "not", "almost", "sometimes", "never".
The small words are not only these five. Also: yet, all, also, nor, anyway, at all, anymore, even if, altogether, other, every bit, a single, any number of, certain (meaning "a specific"), could, most probably; the speaker's own frame ("I would say"); an adjective on a key noun ("compulsive gambling" - అదుపులేని జూదం); and steps of degree ("pretty strong" is కాస్త ఎక్కువ బలంగానే, not చాలా శక్తివంతంగా).
Also own (సొంత), even (ఆఖరికి, -ఐనా), "a certain level" (ఒక నిర్దిష్ట స్థాయి, not కొంత), "so-called", and comparatives ("much deeper" - మరింత లోతుగా).
But do not flag as omitted a word whose sense the Telugu already carries, or an emphatic doublet that adds nothing in Telugu ("the cells… at the cellular level", "Something, somehow"). When the English explains an Indian word for English readers ("Liberation, or mukti"; "Vasana literally means smell"), the Telugu says the Indian word once (ముక్తి) - the dropped explanation is not an omission.

11. Additions
Something in the Telugu that is not in the English and changes or colours what is being said.
This includes small colourings that read as harmless fluency:
- an intensifier or adjective the English does not have - ఎంతో, నిజమైన, అసలైన, నిరంతర, నిత్య, పూర్తి, ప్రత్యేకమైన, మరింత, తగినంత, కేవలం ("It is not love" is అది ప్రేమ కాదు, not అది నిజమైన ప్రేమ కాదు)
- an emphatic -ఏ (కోసమే, నాతోనే, శరీరంలోనే) where the English has no "only", "itself" or "very" and no contrast. A contrastive -ఏ ("you… not the people around you" - మీరే) is not an addition.
- "tries to" added to a plain verb ("it will run away" is పారిపోతుంది, not పారిపోవడానికి ప్రయత్నిస్తుంది)
- two renderings of one English word joined by లేదా
- స్థితి, అనుభూతి or another interpreting word added to a plain English noun
- a word that interprets the English ("many things may come up" is ఎన్నో విషయాలు ఎదురుకావచ్చు, not ఎన్నో అడ్డంకులు రావచ్చు)
- an explanation inside the text ("nervous energy" is నాడీ శక్తి, not నరాల ఉద్రిక్తతకు కారణమయ్యే నాడీ శక్తి), "only" said twice (ఏకైక … మాత్రమే), and a spoken restatement said twice

12. Meaning shifts
The Telugu reads well but says something different from the English: the wrong subject or object, a reversed or lost negation, a different tense or condition, a cause turned into an effect, a statement turned into a question or the other way round, a figurative expression taken literally.
Check mood as well as tense: "can be" (కాగలదు) is not "is"; "don't" (అందుకోరు) is not "can't" (అందుకోలేరు); "are" is not "may be" (కావచ్చు); "the way is there" (ఉంది) is not "will be seen" (కనిపిస్తుంది); "by now" (ఈపాటికి) is not "at least now" (ఇప్పటికైనా). Aspect too: "is yet to happen" - జరిగి ఉండదు; "should have written" - రాసుంటే. And the force of "should" and "one": "it should be me" (it is bound to be me) - అది నేనే అయి ఉండాలి, not అది నేనే కావాలి (a wish); "This is one reason" - ఇది ఒక కారణం, not అందుకే ("that is why").
Check force as well as sense - the Telugu word must be as blunt or as mild as the English: "slaughter" వధించడం, not బలి ఇవ్వడం; "it invades you" ఆక్రమించేస్తుంది, not ఆవరించేస్తుంది; "nonsense" చెత్త; "starve yourself" మాడ్చుకోవడం. But "some people will run away" (from the ashram) వెళ్లిపోవచ్చు, not పారిపోవచ్చు; "becoming corrupt" దారి తప్పుతున్నాడని, not భ్రష్టుడైపోతున్నాడని.

13. Terms

WHAT NOT TO FLAG
The reviewer put the original back for these, so do not report them:
- A free or idiomatic rendering that makes the same point as the English in its own way - a Telugu idiom, a different image, a word that fits the context, a small link to the next sentence. Before calling something a meaning shift, ask whether a Telugu reader would come away with a different idea than an English reader. If not, leave it, even when the words differ. This protects a free rendering - not a literal rendering of an English figure or set phrase that reads as translated.
- A correct word swapped for a near-synonym only because it is closer to the English or has a slightly different shade. "Fits better", "more natural" or "slightly" is not a reason. But a word in a different sense is not a shade - flag it however small the difference looks (ఇప్పటికీ, "even now", for "still" meaning "nevertheless"; పట్టీ, "strip", for "bundle"). And flag a word when the English pairs it with another on the page ("shallow" and "depth" - లోతు లేని, లోతు).
- THIS BOOK'S TERMS and other settled renderings (ప్రత్యక్ష గురువు, పంచభూతాలు, జనన మరణ చక్రాలు). Never flag them as formal, technical or Sanskritised.
- మీరు, మీ, మిమ్మల్ని, మీకు and the మీరు verb endings for "you" (type 15).
- An English word in brackets after a Sadhguru concept, a coined or technical name, a foreign custom, product or drug, or a pun - కృప (Grace), ప్రతిష్ఠాపన (consecration), లోలకం (pendulum), (Marlboro man). - A -కొను verb for something that happens to one's own body (ఎముకలు విరగొట్టుకునే).

LANGUAGE
Write clear, natural, modern written Telugu - the language of a good Telugu book today.
